# 荒诞 DV 街头

Create a candid record of a world whose residents accept the impossible as routine. The humor comes from the gap between what is happening and how little anyone cares.

## Choose the deliverable

- Generate images directly when the user asks to create or generate images. Use the available image-generation tool and make one call per requested independent image.
- Write prompts only when the user asks for prompts or names a target generator such as Midjourney or 即梦. Preserve requested parameters verbatim.
- Default to 9:16 independent images with no collage when the user gives no aspect ratio and the conversation concerns short-video or street imagery.
- For a 9:16 result, retain the visual logic of footage crudely reframed or cropped from a 4:3 consumer camcorder source. Avoid the polished composition and clarity of modern vertical phone video.

## Generate ideas through open mutation

Never sample from a finite scene menu. Treat every example from the user or prior outputs as a clue to the tone and, unless the user explicitly requests it, place its central combination on the temporary do-not-repeat list.

For every batch, silently perform this process:

1. Build a novelty ledger from visible conversation history. Record recently used pairings of central entity, main action, location, absurdity mechanism, and punchline. Ban repeated pairings and superficial swaps.
2. Invent at least four times as many candidate relationships as the requested image count.
3. Begin every candidate with concrete nouns visible in the real world and a familiar action that can be shown in one frame. Build the surprise by putting the wrong real actor, mount, passenger, seat, tool, destination, workplace, or piece of infrastructure into that action.
4. Mutate two to four independent aspects of each candidate: who acts, what familiar action occurs, what receives the action, which tangible tool is used, where it happens, and which ordinary social convention continues around it. These are creative dimensions, not option lists; invent new relationship changes when the scene suggests them.
5. Cross multiple unrelated domains, but make them interact through a clear action and consequence. A strange object merely standing in the street is not enough.
6. Discard the first predictable association, familiar internet-surrealist cliché, simple mascot gag, and any candidate that differs from a recent result only by replacing one animal or prop.
7. Select the candidates with the strongest one-glance readability, most ordinary human response, most unexpected relationship, and clearest physical staging.

For a multi-image request, build a batch diversity matrix before selecting ideas. Give each candidate a different entry in every principal category: scene archetype, specific location, central subject type, main action, dominant absurd object or creature, supporting prop, ordinary bystander activity, spatial mechanism, and visual punchline. If two candidates share a principal noun, location family, action family, or compositional setup, replace one rather than disguising the repetition with color, breed, model, clothing, or scale changes. Common incidental street necessities such as pavement, walls, doors, or pedestrians do not count as repetition unless they become a focal element.

Randomness must affect the concept, verb, spatial relationship, people, dry-weather atmosphere, camera accident, and visual artifact pattern. Repeating the same request in a later turn should produce a substantially different conceptual family.

Allow the imagination to move beyond named props while keeping every visible ingredient literal and recognizable. Any real object, living creature, occupation, routine, vehicle, machine, building, or piece of public infrastructure may participate. The impossible part must be a concrete relationship between those ingredients: an unlikely rider and mount, a normal activity in an impossible seat, an everyday customer entering by an absurd vehicle, a real object used in the wrong practical role, or another newly invented relationship.

## Keep reality factual and absurdity concrete

Use an approximately 85/15 balance:

- About 85 percent of the frame follows ordinary documentary reality: correct animal anatomy, recognizable vehicles and shops, believable clothing, familiar human gestures, normal weather, accurate street materials, plausible scale, and coherent light.
- About 15 percent contains one dominant violation: a concrete subject performs or enables an action that would be startling or impossible in real life.

The viewer should first register a believable street recording and then notice that the central relationship makes no sense. Removing the one absurd relationship should leave an ordinary documentary scene.

Use the user's examples only to calibrate the rule: riding a tiger while shopping, calmly taking a selfie while a hat burns, sitting on a flying carpet, eating on an aircraft wing, an alpaca walking on stilts, or entering a shop on a camel. These work because every noun and action is immediately recognizable even though their combination is unreasonable. Do not automatically repeat these examples.

Reject:

- invisible or abstract concepts treated as merchandise, material, weather, or labor;
- poetic metaphors made literal, such as weighing gravity, washing a cloud, repairing time, sweeping sunlight, or bottling silence;
- creatures or machines with invented anatomy that cannot be identified at a glance;
- complicated dream symbolism that requires a caption to explain;
- a normal street with an unrelated giant object pasted into the background;
- many independent impossibilities competing in one frame.

Prefer a scene that can be described as one literal sentence using common nouns and an active verb. The sentence may be impossible, but it must describe visible physical things.

## Construct a readable absurd event

Give each image:

- One unmistakable central event with a recognizable subject, familiar active verb, tangible target, and visible consequence.
- One mundane human task continuing inside or immediately beside that event.
- Calm bystanders whose attention remains on shopping, eating, waiting, repairing, carrying, chatting, working, or another situation-specific activity.
- Zero to two second-order details caused by the central event. Keep the rest of the background normal.
- Credible physical evidence: weight, contact shadows, surface deformation, ropes, brackets, dust displacement, dry footprints or tire marks, wheel loading, landing supports, or another scene-specific clue. Use water behavior or wet reflections only when wet conditions are explicitly requested or water is the central subject.
- A camera position that feels accidental: foreground blockage, poor timing, partial cropping, tilted horizon, late zoom, missed focus, or a passerby entering the frame.

Make the impossible element perform, carry, enter, sit, wait, eat, shop, commute, work, or otherwise participate in a familiar physical routine rather than appearing as a novelty prop. Favor simple verbs and instantly legible body positions.

Keep one visual anchor dominant. Secondary oddities should reward a closer look without turning the frame into a crowded “spot the object” puzzle.

## Preserve deadpan behavior

Everyone treats the situation as normal. Use neutral expressions, relaxed posture, unfinished gestures, casual clothing, and attention directed toward the task instead of the camera. Passersby continue moving and do not stare, point, scream, celebrate, or raise phones.

Let reactions remain socially believable even when physics is not. A vendor still counts change, a commuter still waits their turn, and a neighbor still finishes a chore.

Avoid invasion, disaster, miracle, heroic spectacle, magical reveal, theme-park staging, or an advertising pose. Keep animals calm and free of injury or distress.

## Ground the place

When the user specifies Chongqing, establish it through several ordinary cues rather than a postcard landmark: steep stair streets, stacked roads, riverside slopes, dense aging residential buildings, security-bar balconies, laundry, air conditioners, sun-faded concrete, food stalls, buses, monorail, bridges, and high-rises fading into haze. Rotate altitude, neighborhood age, street width, time, lighting, and proximity to the river between images.

For another named place, replace these cues with locally credible daily details. If no place is given, infer it from the conversation; otherwise use a plausible Chinese street environment.

Keep the environment prominent. Prefer environmental wide shots or middle-distance frames over close portraits so the absurd spatial relationship can be understood.

### Keep the ground dry by default

Unless the user explicitly requests rain, wet weather, puddles, flooding, or a just-rained atmosphere, show dry pavement and dry steps with matte, dusty, chalky, sun-faded, or worn surface texture. Do not add rain, drizzle, puddles, wet asphalt, mirror-like road reflections, dripping eaves, umbrellas, water splashes, or the stock blue-gray “rainy cinematic street” treatment. Haze, overcast skies, river mist, fluorescent night light, or humid air may appear without making the ground wet. In a batch, dryness remains the stable default rather than a weather variation.

## Add restrained dreamcore unease

Treat 梦核 as familiar reality remembered incorrectly, not as decorative fantasy. Build it through one or two quiet cues that support the central event: a transitional space that feels strangely vacant, an obsolete public sign, a corridor or stairway that seems to lead somewhere slightly wrong, an institutional pastel color, a too-still pocket of daylight, mismatched scale, an old playground or waiting area appearing in an implausible but physical place, or another newly invented memory-like discontinuity.

Keep dreamcore subordinate to the concrete absurd action and authentic DV recording. Favor liminal familiarity, spatial wrongness, childhood-memory residue, dead air, repetition inside the environment, and unexplained calm. Avoid glowing portals, cosmic skies, floating dream symbols, neon fantasy fog, supernatural auras, ornate magic, horror monsters, or a generic vaporwave/pastel filter. The frame should still look like a real location captured accidentally; the unease comes from what is physically there and how ordinary everyone finds it.

Across a batch, do not reuse the same dreamcore cue or liminal-space family. For example, if one image uses an obsolete waiting room, no other image should reuse a waiting area, rows of seats, or the same institutional-memory mechanism.

## Enforce authentic DV acquisition

Write the prompt as a frame captured by an early-2000s consumer MiniDV camcorder. Put that acquisition statement near the beginning, not only in the style suffix.

Use the underlying behavior of PAL-era 4:3 DV as the visual reference:

- 720×576-class low-detail image, 25 fps interlaced video, tiny-sensor deep focus, consumer zoom lens, flat available light;
- electronic edge enhancement with faint halos, jagged diagonal edges, block compression, mosquito noise, and coarse chroma resolution;
- visible combing on moving hands, legs, vehicles, water, or fabric; slight motion smear from ordinary camcorder shutter behavior;
- clipped white sky and lamps, muddy or crushed dark areas, weak highlight roll-off, and limited dynamic range;
- autofocus hunting, focus breathing, abrupt auto-exposure correction, unstable automatic white balance, or cheap digital zoom;
- occasional horizontal tape dropout, bottom-edge head-switching noise, frame jitter, or color-channel displacement.

Select four to seven artifacts that fit the shot. Vary the artifact combination and severity across a batch. Keep the central action recognizable through the degradation.

DV noise is electronic and blocky rather than photographic grain. Exclude 35 mm film grain, film scratches, light leaks, creamy bokeh, shallow depth of field, lens-flare glamour, HDR, crisp 4K detail, teal-orange grading, commercial lighting, and polished cinematic composition unless the user explicitly asks for them.

Keep color cool-neutral, gray-green, fluorescent-shifted, or slightly magenta-blue according to the location light. Avoid automatic sepia or yellow nostalgia.

For still images, describe “a paused interlaced video frame” rather than a vintage photograph. For video, add small hand tremor, delayed reframing, autofocus search, brief exposure pumping, built-in microphone ambience, and no background music unless requested.

## Maintain variety across a batch

For N outputs:

- Do not reuse the same scene archetype or location family. A stair street, convenience store, bus interior, rooftop, underpass, school corridor, market, clinic entrance, riverside path, and residential courtyard are different families; changing one shop sign or moving to another stairway is not enough.
- Do not reuse principal visible elements across outputs: central people or occupations, featured animal species, vehicle class, machine, furniture type, oversized or impossible object, hand-held prop, signage motif, or focal infrastructure. Treat synonyms and near-neighbor substitutions as repeats when they play the same visual role.
- Do not reuse a central entity–verb pair, action family, location–mechanism pair, mundane bystander activity, dreamcore cue, spatial staging, or punchline structure.
- Vary how impossibility works instead of merely changing the prop.
- Vary the density and role of people, camera height, lens zoom, foreground obstruction, time, lighting source, and DV defect pattern while keeping the ground dry unless wet conditions were requested.
- Vary the concrete relationship: who rides what, who sits where, what enters which everyday space, what object performs which familiar job, and how a normal routine continues around it. Invent beyond these grammatical examples.
- Compare every selected idea with recent outputs and replace anything that feels like a reskin.

Before delivery, compare all outputs side by side. If any two could be summarized with the same main nouns, same venue type, same verb, or same visual joke, rewrite one from a different conceptual family. The batch should feel like unrelated moments found on the same mysterious tape, not variations from a prompt grid.

## Construct prompts

Describe each image in this order:

1. consumer MiniDV acquisition and ordinary location conditions;
2. central absurd action and visible consequence;
3. calm human behavior and small supporting details;
4. physical staging and environmental evidence;
5. accidental framing and selected DV artifacts;
6. exclusions.

State who or what is doing what, to whom or to what, with which physical support, and how surrounding life continues. The word “surreal” alone is insufficient.

End generation prompts with constraints suited to the request. By default require realistic live-action footage, an independent image, no collage, no staged posing, no exaggerated reaction, no glossy science-fiction concept art, no fantasy lighting, no typography, no subtitle, no watermark, no logo, and no timestamp.

## Check before delivery

Confirm every output passes these tests:

1. **Immediate action:** the central event can be stated as a specific active sentence.
2. **Deadpan life:** someone continues a mundane task and nobody performs for the camera.
3. **Fresh relationship:** it does not reuse a recent entity–verb, location–mechanism, or punchline pairing.
4. **Physical evidence:** support, weight, shadows, dust, fabric, surface wear, or other materials make the impossible event feel recorded; water cues appear only when requested or central to the event.
5. **True DV frame:** deep focus, electronic degradation, interlaced motion behavior, limited dynamic range, and amateur framing outweigh modern cinematic polish.
6. **Factual ingredients:** every subject and object has a recognizable real-world appearance; only their relationship is unreasonable.
7. **No explanation needed:** the visual joke is understood without a caption, metaphor, or invisible concept.
8. **Dry-ground default:** no wet surface, rain cue, puddle, or reflective asphalt appears unless requested.
9. **Batch-wide uniqueness:** no other output repeats its scene family, principal visible elements, action family, dreamcore cue, spatial mechanism, or punchline.
10. **Dreamcore restraint:** the image contains memory-like or liminal unease without fantasy spectacle or losing documentary credibility.

Rewrite or regenerate anything that is merely a large object in a street, an abstract concept made visible, a glossy fantasy illustration, a clean high-resolution photograph with a noise overlay, a familiar meme with a swapped animal, or a cluttered collection of disconnected oddities.
